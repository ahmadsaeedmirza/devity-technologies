Progressive Web Apps and native apps both solve real problems well, and the right choice for a B2B product depends on genuine, specific factors rather than a general preference for one approach. This guide covers PWA strengths and real limits, native strengths, honest cost and reach, and the decision factors that actually matter.

| | PWA | Native |
|---|---|---|
| Codebases to maintain | One | Separate per platform, or one via React Native |
| Distribution | Instant, via browser | App store submission and review |
| Device access | Limited | Full |
| Best fit | Most B2B tools, employee-installed | Products needing deep hardware integration |

## PWA Strengths and Limits

**A single codebase reaches users across desktop and mobile, installable directly from a browser**, without app store submission or review, a genuine advantage for speed to market and ongoing maintenance, one codebase to update rather than separate native builds.

**PWAs have real, honest limits worth understanding upfront.** Deep integration with certain device hardware, some background processing capabilities, and full offline functionality for complex, data-heavy applications remain genuinely stronger on native, a PWA that needs to fight against these limits is usually a sign native is the better fit.

**Browser and platform support for PWA capabilities varies**, and while support has genuinely improved, it's worth confirming the specific capabilities your product needs are well supported across the browsers your actual users rely on, not assumed uniformly available.

![PWA strengths, one codebase, installable, no app store review](/images/blog/pwa-strengths-diagram.svg)

## Native Strengths

**Full access to device capabilities**, camera, sensors, background processing, deep hardware integration, remains genuinely stronger on native, a real requirement for a product whose core value depends on capabilities a browser-based app cannot fully replicate.

**App store presence** offers real discovery and distribution value for products that benefit from it, though this matters considerably less for a B2B tool used by employees told to install it, than for a consumer product relying on organic app store discovery.

**Performance for genuinely complex, graphics-intensive, or offline-heavy applications** tends to be stronger on native, though for the large majority of standard B2B business applications, this gap has narrowed considerably and matters less than it once did.

## Cost and Reach

**A PWA is generally cheaper to build and maintain**, one codebase covering both desktop and mobile web, compared to native development, which typically means separate iOS and Android builds, or a cross-platform framework like React Native still requiring more specialised, separate app store submission and maintenance.

**Reach differs meaningfully by context.** A PWA reaches anyone with a browser instantly, no install friction beyond a single tap, native apps require an app store download, real friction for a first-time user, though native offers a more integrated, polished experience once installed.

![PWA versus native cost and reach, one codebase versus device capability](/images/blog/pwa-native-cost-reach-diagram.svg)

## Decision Factors

**Does your product genuinely need deep device capability access?** If the core value proposition depends on hardware integration a browser cannot provide, native is the right, non-negotiable choice, not a preference.

**Does app store discovery genuinely matter for your specific product?** A B2B tool where users are told to use it by their employer typically doesn't need this distribution channel the way a consumer app does, a real, honest factor worth weighing rather than defaulting to native for perceived legitimacy alone.

**What's your realistic maintenance capacity?** A PWA's single codebase is genuinely easier to maintain long-term for a smaller team, native's separate platform builds require either more resourcing or a cross-platform framework to manage that overhead sensibly.

**How do your actual users access the product?** A workforce primarily on desktop browsers during work hours, needing the tool available instantly without an install process, is well served by a PWA. A workforce genuinely mobile-first, needing offline access in the field or deep integration with a device's camera or sensors, points more clearly toward native or a genuine cross-platform mobile build.
